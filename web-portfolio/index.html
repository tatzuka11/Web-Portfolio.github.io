Becoming a Professional IT Practitioner

Sean Christler C. Abanales · BSIT 4th Year · J.H. Cerilles State College

Professionalism in IT is more than technical skill — it is about integrity, responsibility, and how we handle the trust placed in us. As someone who has built systems like the Student Violation Alert System at JHCSC, I have seen firsthand that writing code means handling real people's data. That responsibility demands ethical conduct: protecting confidentiality, implementing proper security, and respecting every user's privacy.

Among the qualities of a professional IT practitioner, I consider reliability my strongest — I follow through on commitments, whether for school projects or part-time work for schoolmates. The area I need to improve is accountability: learning to own my mistakes openly rather than quietly fixing them. Professionalism also extends to digital behavior — our social media presence and online conduct shape our reputation long before any interview. If I saw a classmate's password written beside their computer, the professional response is to educate them privately about the risk, not ignore it or exploit it.

Technology evolves rapidly, which is why continuous learning is essential. After graduation, I plan to deepen my skills in Laravel, explore frameworks like React, and learn more about cybersecurity. The 5 R's — Responsibility, Respect, Reliability, Responsibility for Ethics, and Readiness to Learn — will guide my growth throughout my career.

"As a future IT professional, I commit myself to three things: first, I will protect the confidentiality and privacy of all data entrusted to me; second, I will embrace continuous learning as a lifelong practice; and third, I will hold myself accountable for my actions, my code, and my professional conduct — both online and offline."
